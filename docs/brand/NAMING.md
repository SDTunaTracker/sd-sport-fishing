# Naming exploration

Status: Phase 2 draft for the owner's review (2026-11-24). The owner decides. Nothing has been bought, registered or filed.

> **These checks are preliminary, not legal clearance.** They come from web searches of trademark mirrors (Justia), the App Store, Google results and public domain registry lookups (RDAP/DNS) on 2026-11-24. They can miss pending filings, common-law use, state marks and anything outside the US. Before committing to any name, get a proper trademark clearance search from a trademark attorney (classes 9 and 42 for the app/software, 35 for the booking/marketplace side, 41 for content).

---

## 1. An honest look at "The Tuna Tracker"

**What works**
- Alliterative and easy to say at the dock. People remember it after one hearing.
- "Tuna" is the emotional hook of SoCal offshore fishing; it signals offshore, not bass ponds.
- No registered "TUNA TRACKER" mark turned up, and no app with that name in the App Store.
- The site, the domain and whatever word of mouth exists all use it.

**What doesn't**
- **Narrower than the product.** The site covers yellowtail, dorado, rockfish trips, boat pages, booking and Co-Captain. A yellowtail regular or a half-day family isn't obviously the audience.
- **"Tracker" points the wrong way.** It suggests GPS tagging, fish finders or "we know where the tuna are". That conflicts with the rule that forecasting must not be marketed as reliable.
- **"The"** makes the app-store name, social handles (@thetunatracker) and search a bit clumsier.
- **Regional growth:** OC/LA is already live in the region toggle. "Tuna" still works up the coast in summer, but less so for winter rockfish and an eventual Central Coast or Pacific Northwest.

**Verdict:** a good, ownable niche name with a scope problem, not a broken name. The scope problem can largely be solved with a descriptor ("Tuna Tracker: SoCal sportboat fish counts") instead of a rename.

## 2. What a rename would cost

| Cost | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SEO equity** | Small to medium | thetunatracker.com was registered 2026-05-21, so it has about six months of history. Most organic traffic for a site like this usually comes from non-branded queries (boat names, "san diego fish counts"), and that survives a domain move if every URL gets a 301. Branded queries and backlinks are the part that's at risk. The SEO agent has been asked for real numbers (see `seo-naming-input.md` in the shared notes folder when it arrives). |
| **Existing users** | Small | Early audience; a "we've grown up, same site" announcement plus permanent redirects covers it. Home-screen PWA installs keep the old icon until reinstalled. |
| **Domain** | Low, but there's a timing issue | **tunatracker.com** (without "the") is in registrar *redemption* status as of today (registered 2026-01-02, now on client hold). Domains in redemption usually return to the open market about 35 days later unless the owner renews. If the owner wants it, a backorder through a registrar is their call; I have not touched it. |
| **App store** | None yet | The app isn't published, so now is the cheapest moment to settle its name. |
| **Brand assets** | Low | The logo is being replaced anyway. |

**Bottom line:** a rename is cheapest *now*, before the app ships. It's still not free, and the current name is not a liability. A rename is worth it only if a candidate is clearly better and clears legal checks.

## 5. Recommendation

**Path 1 (recommended): evolve, don't rename.** Call the product **Tuna Tracker** (drop "The" in the logo, app and copy), keep thetunatracker.com, and carry scope in a descriptor: *Tuna Tracker · SoCal sportboat fish counts*. It keeps all equity, fixes most of the narrowness, and costs nothing but the new logo. Optionally, the owner can backorder tunatracker.com while it's in redemption and 301 it to the main site.

**Path 2: if you want a name built for growth, Railside is the strongest new name.** It's distinctive, premium, true to sportboat culture, species-neutral, and nothing turned up in fishing. It needs a domain strategy (railsidefishing.com is open; railside.com would be a purchase from its owner), a real trademark search, and the rename plan in section 6.

Avoid Wide Open (crowded marks). Foamer and Rail Count are fun backups; Sportboat HQ is the safe-but-plain fallback.

## 6. If a new name is picked: rename plan (outline)

Coordinated with the "SEO for thetunatracker.com" session before anything happens.

1. **Clear it:** attorney trademark search; secure the domain(s) and handles (owner does all purchases and sign-ups).
2. **Build:** new logo set and app name on the brand branch; keep URL paths identical.
3. **Domain move:** new domain serves the site; thetunatracker.com returns a 301 for every path to the same path on the new domain (Cloudflare redirect rule or `_redirects`). Keep the old domain renewed indefinitely.
4. **SEO:** update canonical tags, sitemap, `og:url`, structured data `name`; Google Search Console change-of-address; keep both properties verified; monitor branded and non-branded clicks for 8 weeks.
5. **App store:** launch under the new name from day one (no rename needed since it isn't published).
6. **Social:** claim matching handles; keep @thetunatracker handles with a pointer.
7. **Announce:** site banner "The Tuna Tracker is now [Name]: same counts, same boats", an email if there's a list, a BloodyDecks post, and a Co-Captain reply for "what happened to Tuna Tracker?".
8. **Afterwards:** keep "formerly The Tuna Tracker" in the footer and About page for 6–12 months.
